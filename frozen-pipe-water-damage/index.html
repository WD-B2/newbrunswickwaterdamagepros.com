A frozen pipe becomes an emergency the moment it splits and thaws. Our line is open 24/7, including cold nights. Shut off the main water valve, keep heat on in the building and stay clear of any wet ceiling or outlet while you wait for us.

Why pipes burst when they thaw, not when they freeze

Ice expands inside a pipe and raises pressure between the ice plug and the closed faucet. The pipe may crack without leaking, because the ice itself seals the crack. Water pours out when the ice melts, often hours later when the heat comes back on or the day warms up.

That timing explains why people come home to a flood after a cold spell. The damage you find is the result of a break that happened earlier.

Where pipes freeze in New Brunswick buildings

Exterior walls

Supply lines run against cold masonry or thin insulation.

Crawl spaces and unheated basements

Pipes sit in cold air with little protection.

Attics and garages

Sprinkler, laundry and bath supply lines pass through unheated space.

Vacant or shut-off units

No heat means no protection when the building cools.

In 2018 the Rutgers NJ Weather Network noted a 14-day run of subfreezing days at New Brunswick during a prolonged cold spell. A stretch like that tests every pipe on an exterior wall.

What to do when you suspect a frozen or split pipe

  1. Close the main valve. This limits water if the pipe has already cracked.
  2. Open the faucet on the frozen line. Open it slightly so melting water has somewhere to go.
  3. Warm the area gently. Use a hair dryer or space heater at a safe distance. Never use an open flame.
  4. Check for wet spots and call. Look at ceilings, wall bases and cabinets below the pipe, then call our line.

What affects the cost of frozen pipe cleanup

Scope drives price. We look at how many rooms are wet, whether water reached lower floors, which materials absorbed it and how long it ran before the valve was closed. We explain the scope and price before work starts. Repairing the pipe is a plumber's job and is separate.

Preventing frozen pipes before the next cold snap

  • Insulate pipes in unheated spaces with foam sleeves.
  • Seal gaps where pipes enter through exterior walls.
  • Keep the heat on in vacant units and set it no lower than a safe minimum.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during extreme cold.
  • Let a faucet drip slightly on the coldest nights.

Know where your main shutoff valve is before winter. If you leave town for several days, ask someone to check the building.

What happens after you find a thawed, leaking pipe

We trace the wet path from the break through walls, ceilings and floors. Water may have run for hours while no one was home, so the affected area can be larger than expected. Moisture readings set the scope, and extraction and drying begin immediately.

Insurance note for frozen pipes

Policies often treat a sudden pipe break differently from slow seepage. Some ask that heat was kept on in the building. Read your policy, report promptly and photograph the damage before cleanup.

For the water that follows a break, see burst pipe water damage. Ceilings that stained below an upper-floor break are covered under ceiling water damage repair. Wall cavities are dried as part of wall and drywall water damage repair.

After a thaw flood, many professionals set drying goals from the IICRC S500 water damage standard. Naming a standard does not replace a written scope, so ask for both.
