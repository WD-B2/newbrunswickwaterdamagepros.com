About the district

The Livingston Avenue Historic District is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. It covers the area around Livingston Avenue near downtown New Brunswick, and the National Park Service dates its period of significance from 1850 to 1949. The district includes Queen Anne, Colonial Revival and Classical Revival buildings, and the register records 58 contributing buildings.

Well known places in and around the district include the Henry Guest House at 58 Livingston Avenue, which dates to 1760, and the New Brunswick Free Public Library. A listing on the register is a recognition of history. It does not by itself decide what you may repair, so check with the city before rebuild work to learn whether any approvals apply.

Why old materials need a different approach

Buildings from the period of the district often have plaster over wood lath, solid wood floors and thick painted trim. Water can pass through plaster and sit behind it, and lath can stay damp longer than the surface suggests. Moisture readings matter more here than how a wall looks.

Saving what can be saved

Professionals often set drying targets from ANSI/IICRC S500, a published standard for water damage work. Asking how a company records its readings tells you more than any logo.

We cannot promise that every material can be saved. What we can do is measure, dry gently and explain the choices. Slow, controlled drying often protects solid wood and plaster better than aggressive heat. If trim has to come off, photograph it first and label the pieces so a carpenter can reinstall it.

Check your policy for how older materials are valued, because some policies pay differently for repair and replacement. A clear photo record and a written scope help your insurer see what was done. They cannot promise a payout.

What to do right now

  1. Shut off the water at the fixture or main valve if you can reach it safely.
  2. Stay away from wet ceilings that sag, and keep lights off in rooms with dripping water.
  3. Photograph the damage, including ceilings, trim and floors.
  4. Call us before tearing anything out so wet plaster and trim can be assessed.
