A downtown that keeps building up

Downtown New Brunswick mixes offices, shops, theaters and apartment buildings around George Street, Livingston Avenue, New Street and Neilson Street. The State Theatre, built in 1921, sits beside much newer towers. In March 2026 the city planning board conditionally approved a 45 story building at 259 George Street with about 800 apartments, according to New York YIMBY.

Tall buildings carry water through risers, sprinkler lines, roof drains and shared mechanical rooms. A failure at one point can send water down many floors, which is why we ask for the floor, the unit and the building contact right away.

How a downtown job differs

Access and timing

Loading docks, elevators and after-hours entry have to be arranged before equipment can be moved in.

Many parties

Owners, managers, tenants and insurers may all need updates. Written records help everyone.

Business continuity

Shops and offices want the area reopened quickly. Drying plans can be staged floor by floor.

Shared systems

Ceiling leaks may come from the unit above, a riser or a roof drain. Finding the source comes first.

Heavy rain and the river side of downtown

Downtown sits close to the Raritan River. When Hurricane Ida hit in 2021, the Daily Targum reported closures on Route 18, at the Albany Street Bridge and on the roads linking Route 18 to Neilson Street. Most downtown water damage is not river flooding, but lower levels and garages near the river side deserve a second look after a storm.

If floodwater enters a basement, garage or lobby, keep people away from the water and from electrical equipment. Floodwater can carry sewage and chemicals, so it should be handled by trained crews with the right protection.

Steps for a manager or tenant

  1. Find the source. Check the unit above, the riser and the roof drain. Close the nearest valve if it is safe.
  2. Protect people. Keep guests and staff away from wet floors and from any area where water meets electrical equipment.
  3. Document. Photograph each affected floor, ceiling and piece of equipment, and note the time.
  4. Call. Give us the address, the floor, the contact for building access and any parking or loading limits.

Commercial properties have their own planning needs, covered on the commercial water damage page. Ceiling and wall damage appear on the ceiling water damage page, and flooded lower levels on the flood damage page.
