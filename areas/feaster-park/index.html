Where Feaster Park is

Feaster Park is a residential neighborhood in the city's Second Ward. The park itself sits between Commercial Avenue and Throop Avenue, and Handy Street separates it from Pittman Park, according to the Rutgers Center for Urban Environmental Sustainability. Paul Robeson Community School for the Arts is nearby, which makes a handy landmark when you give directions.

The park is about five acres and was renamed in 1937 for Joseph Feaster, the city's director of streets and public improvements, who lived on Throop Avenue. A redesign reopened it in September 2023. Neighborhood data summaries describe the area as densely populated, which matters for how a leak travels between homes.

Everyday causes we hear about

In a densely built area, a small failure can touch several households. A cracked toilet supply line, a washing machine hose, a leaking dishwasher or an old water heater can soak a floor in minutes. The damage often spreads under flooring and into the ceiling below before anyone sees it.

Supply lines and valves

Braided hoses and shutoff valves wear out. Closing the nearest valve is the first move if it is safe to reach.

Water heaters

Basement units can leak slowly for weeks. Look for rust, puddles and a damp smell near the base.

Drains and toilets

A slow drain that suddenly backs up can mean a blockage further along the line. Avoid using fixtures until it is checked.

Roof and window leaks

Heavy rain can find a weak spot around flashing or a window frame. Stains that grow after storms point to this.

What a dry-out looks like

A dry-out has three parts. First, standing water is removed. Second, wet walls, floors and cavities are measured with moisture meters. Third, air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings return to normal for the materials. The EPA's guidance is to dry wet areas within 24 to 48 hours and to hold indoor humidity under 60 percent after the work is done.

Carpet pad, soaked insulation and drywall that has stayed wet are often removed because they dry poorly. Hardwood and plaster are judged case by case. Our structural drying page explains how readings guide those choices.

Before the crew arrives

Move small valuables and papers to a dry room.

Do not use ceiling fixtures under a wet ceiling.

Keep a list of damaged items with photos for your insurer.

Insurance and paperwork

Coverage turns on the cause of the damage and the wording of your policy, so read it and call your insurer early. A sudden pipe or appliance failure is usually handled differently from rising water outside, and your insurer can explain how your policy treats each.

Ask for an itemized scope in writing before work starts. For rebuild work after the dry-out, get the contractor's state registration number and a scope on paper before you sign anything.
