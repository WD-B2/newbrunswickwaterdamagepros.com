The riverfront side of the city

Elmer B. Boyd Memorial Park is a 20 acre municipal park along the Raritan River in New Brunswick, reopened in 1999 after a rebuild. The Lower Raritan Watershed Partnership describes the park floodplain as an area that floods repeatedly and acts as a sponge that protects other parts of the city. Townhomes and apartments stand along Route 18 nearby.

This page covers the streets near the park, including the John Street Extension and Riverside Drive area. We do not claim exact boundaries, so call and tell us your street.

What has been reported here

News 12 reported in January 2024 that signs on the John Street Extension and Riverside Drive tell residents to move cars to high ground during heavy rain. The station said storm drains there fill quickly, and that cars and garages can take on water. It also said townhouses and apartments along Route 18 tend to suffer property damage when the river spills over the road.

The largest recent event was Hurricane Ida in September 2021. According to the City of New Brunswick, 8 to 8.4 inches of rain fell, the Raritan overtopped its banks and Route 18 closed. Patch reported more than 1,000 people were evacuated across the city.

Floodwater safety first

  • Do not wade into standing floodwater. Hidden hazards and sewage are possible.
  • Do not touch electrical equipment or breakers while standing in water.
  • Keep kids and animals clear of the water and of any soaked debris.
  • Do not try to dry or clean floodwater with household fans or a vacuum.
  • Call 911 or the utility if there is a gas smell, sparks or fast moving water.

Once it is safe, photograph the damage and the water line, then call us. Floodwater is treated as contaminated, which affects what can be saved. Porous materials such as carpet pad and drywall that sat in floodwater are usually removed.

Insurance and flood maps

The New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance notes that flood is generally left out of a standard homeowners policy. People who want it buy a separate flood policy. Ask your insurer how your policy treats rain that leaks in through a roof or window compared with river water that rises from outside.

FEMA publishes its flood maps online and you can search them by street address. A map zone is only an estimate of risk and it does not decide whether water reaches a given building.
