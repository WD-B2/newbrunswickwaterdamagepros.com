Finding the Fifth Ward when you call

The Fifth Ward is the part of New Brunswick that runs along French Street and Somerset Street, close to downtown and the Raritan River. Naming a cross street is the quickest way to tell us where the water is. Landmarks help too, such as Robert Wood Johnson University Hospital near French Street or Mindszenty Square at Somerset and Plum Streets.

The ward has a strong Hungarian heritage. By 1930, Hungarian residents made up between one fifth and one third of the city, according to the Clio history tour of New Brunswick, and many opened businesses along French and Somerset Streets. The Hungarian Festival still takes place on Somerset Street each June, so we ask about street access when an event is near.

Why shared walls change the plan

Housing data summaries describe many Fifth Ward homes as built before 1940 and most households as renters. Age alone does not decide how much damage a leak causes, but it tells us what to expect inside the walls, such as plaster, old wood floors and cast iron drains.

In a rowhouse or small apartment building, water from a kitchen or bathroom follows floor joists and pipe chases. It can show up as a stain two units away. We ask which floors and units are wet before we send equipment, because the dry-out has to follow the water, not just the visible stain.

What to do in the first hour

  1. Shut off the water if you can reach the valve safely. Use the fixture valve or the main. If water has reached outlets or the electrical panel, stay back and phone the utility or 911.
  2. Photograph the damage. Take wide shots and close shots of walls, ceilings and belongings before anything is moved.
  3. Tell the building owner or manager in writing. A text or email creates a record with a time and date.
  4. Call us. We explain what extraction and drying involve and what the scope covers before work starts.

Renters, owners and insurance

A renters policy and the building owner's policy are separate. One may cover your belongings and the other the structure, depending on the terms, so check both and ask each insurer what it needs. Dated photos and a written scope make a claim easier to explain, though neither one guarantees approval.

If water comes up through a floor drain or toilet, treat it as contaminated. Keep children and pets away and do not try to clean it with a household vacuum. Our sewage backup cleanup page explains the safer process.
