Water in an office, shop, restaurant or apartment building is a business emergency. Our line is open 24/7. While you wait, shut off the source if you can, move records and electronics off floors, and keep customers and staff away from wet floors and ceilings.

Commercial water damage differs from residential jobs

A commercial loss involves more than wet drywall. There are tenants, leases, inventory, ceiling grids, shared plumbing and business hours. A leak on the fourth floor can reach several units below, and each one may have a different insurer.

We plan commercial jobs around keeping the business running where possible, working in phases and protecting areas that are still in use.

Emergency commercial water damage: what to do first

  1. Make the area safe. Block off wet floors and keep people away from sagging ceiling tiles.
  2. Stop the source. Close the nearest shutoff or the main building valve.
  3. Protect records and equipment. Move paper, electronics and stock to dry areas.
  4. Call us and your property manager. Tell us the building type, the floors affected and whether tenants are on site.

What affects commercial water damage costs

Costs depend on the size of the space, the number of floors, the finishes, the amount of equipment and the hours available for work. Tenant coordination and phased work also affect the plan. We explain the scope and price before work starts.

Planning around tenants, leases and business hours

In commercial buildings, the restoration plan has to fit how the space is used. We agree on work hours, access routes and noise limits with the manager or owner. Areas that stay open are separated from work zones with barriers and air control.

Lease terms often decide who pays for what, so property managers should check responsibilities for the structure, the tenant improvements and contents. Written notes of conversations help when insurers from several parties get involved.

Records a business should collect right away

  • Dated photos and video of affected areas and equipment.
  • An inventory of damaged stock, furniture and electronics.
  • Records of lost days or closed hours.
  • Contact details for tenants and neighboring units.
  • Copies of your policy, including business interruption terms.

Insurance and documentation

Business property and business interruption coverage vary by policy. Keep photos, moisture logs and inventory lists. Read your policy and talk with your insurer or broker before cleanup.

We start with water extraction where water is standing, then structural drying. Sewage events follow sewage backup cleanup. For a basement level, see basement water damage cleanup.

ANSI/IICRC S500 applies to commercial water losses as well as homes. Property managers can ask a company how its crews follow it in occupied buildings.
