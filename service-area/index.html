What makes New Brunswick different for water damage

The Raritan River is the city's flood driver. During Hurricane Ida in 2021 it overtopped its banks, and the city was among the places in the region that saw evacuations. Even without a river flood, heavy rain can raise ground moisture and fill low basements.

The city is also a dense mix of older homes, apartment buildings, student rentals, hospitals, university buildings and downtown offices. A leak in a shared building often affects neighbors below, so we plan around access, tenants and managers.

Major routes and getting to a job

US 1, Route 18, Route 27 and the New Jersey Turnpike all pass through or near the city. Closures on the river roads during heavy rain can change how a job is reached, and we ask about access on the phone.

Neighborhoods and nearby towns

Within the city, people call from areas such as the Fifth Ward, Feaster Park, the Livingston Avenue Historic District, downtown New Brunswick, the Boyd Park riverfront, Raritan Gardens, Edgebrook and Westons Mills. Nearby towns include South River, Highland Park, Piscataway, North Brunswick, Edison, East Brunswick, Milltown, Somerset and Sayreville.

Each town has its own building mix and its own building department for permit questions. For work inside New Brunswick, ask the city's Construction Department.

Why local conditions matter to a restoration plan

A flooded basement near the river calls for a different plan than a roof leak in a downtown apartment building. Access, parking, building rules and neighbors all affect how a crew works. We ask about them on the phone so equipment and people are ready.

Older buildings often have plaster, original hardwood and wall cavities with little fire blocking, so moisture can travel farther than expected. Newer buildings use drywall and engineered flooring, which respond differently to water. The right plan depends on the construction as much as on the water.

Parking, access and building rules

Many jobs in the city are in multi-unit buildings, where a crew needs a loading spot, an elevator or a building contact. Tell us if the building has a doorman, a managed entrance or rules about work hours. Having a manager on the phone saves time.

In smaller towns, driveways and street parking are usually simpler, and basement access is often through a bulkhead or an interior stair. Mention any narrow stairs or tight crawl space entries so we send the right equipment.

What to do and what to tell us on the call

  • The street or town where the water is.
  • The source if you know it, such as a pipe, appliance, roof or river.
  • Which floors are wet and how deep the water is.
  • Whether anything electrical is wet.
  • Whether anyone is in the building, such as tenants.
