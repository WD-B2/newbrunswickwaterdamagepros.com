Before we arrive

What to do in the first hour of water damage

  1. Get everyone to a dry, safe spot. Stay out of rooms where water has reached outlets, appliances or the electrical panel. If you can reach the main breaker without stepping in water, switch off power to the wet area.
  2. Stop the source if it is safe. Close the main water valve, the shutoff under the fixture or the water heater supply. A frozen or hidden leak may keep running until a valve is closed.
  3. Photograph the damage. Take wide and close photos of the water, the ceiling and the affected belongings before anything is moved. Your insurer will ask for them.
  4. Call the emergency line. Our line is open 24/7. Tell us what caused the water, how deep it is and which floors are wet. We explain the next step and the scope before work starts.
  5. Protect what you can carry. Lift small furniture, rugs and electronics onto dry surfaces, and open cabinet doors to let air in. Skip the household vacuum and stay clear of any ceiling that sags.

How the Raritan River and city housing shape water damage here

New Brunswick sits on the south bank of the Raritan River, and the river is the city's main flood story. During Hurricane Ida in 2021, the Raritan overtopped its banks after 8 to 8.4 inches of rain fell on the city, according to the City of New Brunswick. Route 18, River Road and the Albany Street Bridge closed, and Rutgers closed Alexander Library.

Patch reported that about 1,000 people were evacuated across New Brunswick and Piscataway. Floodwater that reaches a basement from outside is not clean water, so it is handled as contaminated until proven otherwise.

Neighborhoods such as the Fifth Ward, Feaster Park, Raritan Gardens, Edgebrook and Westons Mills face the same mix of problems as the rest of the city. Heavy rain seeps into basements, supply lines fail, water heaters leak and drains back up.

The city's tap water is surface water supplied by the New Brunswick Water Utility. A leak from a clean supply line is treated differently from floodwater or a sewer backup, and sorting that out is the first thing we do on the phone.

Insurance and your water damage claim

Standard homeowners policies in New Jersey generally exclude flood damage. Flood coverage needs a separate policy through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private insurer, according to the New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance. Check your policy for what it covers, and ask your insurer about mold coverage before you file.

Hiring a contractor

Hiring a water damage contractor in New Jersey

New Jersey requires home improvement contractors to register with the Division of Consumer Affairs. Registration numbers begin with 13VH, written contracts are required for work above $500, and registration is not an endorsement of quality. A contractor who asks for more than a third of the payment before work begins is a warning sign.

Two published standards guide professional work. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers water damage restoration and S520 covers mold remediation. Ask any company whether its crews work to them.

When repair or rebuild work is part of your water damage job, ask for the contractor's registration number and a written scope. For permit questions, ask the city's Construction Department.
