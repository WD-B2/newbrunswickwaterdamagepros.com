Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Mold? New Brunswick, NJ

Mold coverage is one of the least predictable parts of a homeowners policy. Check yours.

By the New Brunswick Water Damage Pros team. Published November 13, 2026.

Mold coverage varies more than almost any other part of a homeowners policy. Some policies cover mold that results from a covered water loss up to a limit. Others exclude it, and some need an added endorsement. The New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance advises policyholders to check their policy for mold coverage.

This is general information and not legal or insurance advice. Your policy wording and your insurer decide.

Why mold coverage is different

Insurers separate the cause of the water from the mold that follows. A covered burst pipe may lead to a covered mold claim, but mold from a long-term leak, humidity or flood may be excluded. Many policies cap mold payments at a set amount.

Common patterns in mold coverage

Situation.Often.What to check.
Mold from a covered sudden water loss.May be covered up to a limit.Mold sublimit in the policy.
Mold from long-term leaks or neglect.Often excluded.Wear, tear and maintenance exclusions.
Mold after flood.Usually follows flood coverage.Flood policy terms.
Mold from high humidity.Often excluded.Humidity or condensation language.
Mold in a rental.Depends on who owns the loss.Landlord policy and renters policy.

What to look for in your policy

  • A section titled mold, fungi or microbial growth.
  • Dollar limits on mold remediation.
  • Conditions requiring you to dry and repair promptly.
  • Exclusions for gradual or repeated seepage.
  • Endorsements you may have added.

How to protect a mold claim

  1. Dry quickly. Failure to mitigate can undermine a claim.
  2. Document the source and timing. Photos, invoices and moisture readings show when water arrived.
  3. Report promptly. Tell your insurer when you find mold, not months later.
  4. Keep drying logs. They show you acted.
  5. Ask questions in writing. Request the mold section of the policy.

Terms to look for: sublimit, exclusion and endorsement

Term.How it shows up in a mold claim.
Sublimit.A cap on mold payments that may be lower than your overall coverage.
Exclusion.A statement that certain causes or types of mold are not covered.
Endorsement.An add-on that may expand or restrict mold coverage.
Ensuing loss.Damage that follows a covered event, which may be covered even if the first cause is not.

The wording of these terms differs by insurer and by policy. Read the section instead of relying on a summary.

Landlords and tenants

In a rental, the landlord's policy generally covers the building, and a tenant's renters policy covers the tenant's belongings. If mold damages your belongings after a covered loss, your renters policy may respond. If the mold comes from a building defect or a leak the landlord has not fixed, your remedies may come from the lease and the law instead of insurance.

Documenting a mold claim

  • Photographs of the growth and of the moisture source.
  • Dates of when you first noticed the problem and when you reported it.
  • Invoices and reports from remediation and any assessor.
  • Moisture readings and drying logs.
  • Communications with the insurer and landlord.

Myths about mold insurance

One myth is that mold is never covered. Some policies cover mold that follows a covered water loss, usually with a cap. Another is that you can wait to see whether mold appears before telling your insurer. Delays can affect a claim.

A third myth is that flood policies cover mold in all cases. Flood policies have their own terms, and you should read them.

Key takeaways

  • Check your policy for mold, fungi and microbial growth language.
  • Look for sublimits and exclusions.
  • Dry fast and keep records.
  • Ask your insurer before you start remediation.

A step-by-step way to read the mold section of your policy

  1. Find the definitions. Look for how the policy defines mold, fungi and microbial growth, since the definition sets what the exclusions cover.
  2. Check for a limit. Note any dollar cap on mold remediation, testing or related living costs.
  3. Read the exceptions. Some exclusions have exceptions for mold that results from a covered cause, such as a sudden pipe break.
  4. Look for conditions. Many policies require prompt reporting and reasonable steps to dry and protect the property.
  5. Write down questions. Take them to your agent and ask for answers in writing.

This takes about fifteen minutes and is worth doing before a loss. After a loss, you will be thankful you already know where the limits sit.

If you cannot find the mold section, ask your agent where mold is addressed. Some policies bury it in an endorsement or in the exclusions list.

A scenario: mold after a washing machine hose failure

A hose bursts while no one is home, and the floor and the lower wall get wet. The owner dries the area within two days, photographs everything and reports the loss. The insurer treats the hose failure as a sudden release, and any resulting mold is handled under the policy's mold terms.

Compare that with a slow drip behind the same washer that goes unnoticed for months. The mold is the same, but the cause is gradual, and the insurer may see it differently. The cause and the timing drive the outcome, and records of both help your case.

Neither scenario is a promise of how a particular claim ends. Your policy and your insurer decide.

Mold coverage questions after flooding in New Brunswick

After Hurricane Ida in 2021, many New Brunswick property owners learned how flood and mold coverage interact. Standard homeowners policies exclude flood, so mold that follows a flood usually depends on whether a separate flood policy applies and what it says.

If you are in or near the Raritan corridor, ask your agent about flood coverage and how mold is treated under each policy you hold.
