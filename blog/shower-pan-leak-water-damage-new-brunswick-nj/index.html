Shower Pan Leak Water Damage in New Brunswick, NJ

A failed shower pan can leak for months before a stain shows up below.

By the New Brunswick Water Damage Pros team. Published November 13, 2026.

A shower pan sits under the tile and holds the water that falls on the floor of the shower. When it cracks, the liner fails or the drain seal lets go, water leaves through the floor of the shower instead of going down the drain. The damage often shows up in the room below.

What a shower pan does and how it fails

The pan or liner is a waterproof layer under mortar and tile. Water that gets past the grout is supposed to drain to the weep holes at the drain. Over time the liner can crack, the seal at the drain can dry out, or a nail from construction can puncture the liner.

Tile and grout are not waterproof by themselves. That is why a shower can look perfect while it leaks.

Signs that your shower pan may be leaking

  • A stain or bubble on the ceiling below the shower.
  • Water that stays in the shower after it is turned off.
  • A musty smell in the bathroom or in the room below.
  • Loose tiles, cracked grout or soft spots near the shower threshold.
  • Water bill increases without another explanation.

A simple flood test before you hire anyone

A plumber can plug the drain and fill the shower floor with an inch or two of water, then mark the level. If the level drops over several hours with nothing else running, the pan is leaking. Do not leave water unattended, and ask a plumber to do the test if you are unsure.

Repair versus drying: two different jobs

A tile or plumbing contractor repairs the shower pan, which often means removing tile and mortar. A restoration company dries the wet materials around it, including the ceiling below, wall cavities and subfloor. Doing the pan repair before drying is finished can seal moisture in.

  1. Stop using the shower. Every use adds water to the structure.
  2. Confirm the leak. A plumber runs a flood test or inspects the drain.
  3. Dry the structure. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the ceiling and wall cavities.
  4. Repair the pan and rebuild. Once readings are normal, the pan, tile and drywall are rebuilt.

Types of shower floors and how they fail

Shower type.How water is contained.Common failure.
Mortar bed with a liner.A flexible liner under the mortar and tile.Cracked or punctured liner, clogged weep holes.
Pre-formed pan.A molded plastic or composite base.Cracks at the drain or seams.
Fiberglass or acrylic stall.A one-piece unit.Cracks and failed caulk at the edges.
Curbless or wet-room.Waterproofing across the whole floor.Poorly sealed seams and corners.

The type of shower affects how the leak is found and repaired. A one-piece unit is usually replaced, and a mortar bed is opened and rebuilt.

What a shower pan repair involves

A contractor removes the tile and mortar, inspects the liner or base, replaces the damaged part, tests the new pan with a flood test and then retiles. The job is disruptive and takes several days, which is another reason to dry the structure first so the new work is not built over wet framing.

Prevention: small habits that protect the pan

  • Reseal grout and recaulk the edges when they crack or shrink.
  • Keep the drain and weep holes clear.
  • Run the exhaust fan during and after showers.
  • Fix loose tiles quickly before water gets under them.
  • Watch the ceiling below for new stains.

Myths about shower leaks

One myth is that new caulk fixes a leaking shower. Caulk seals edges and joints but cannot repair a failed pan. Another is that leaks only matter when they show on the ceiling below. By then, wood framing and insulation may already be wet, and hidden moisture can lead to mold.

A third myth is that a sound tile surface means a sound shower. Tile is not waterproof, and the layers below do the sealing.

What to remember

  • Stop using a shower you suspect is leaking.
  • Confirm with a flood test before tearing anything apart.
  • Dry the structure before rebuilding.
  • Check your policy for how hidden leaks are treated.

Choosing between repair and full replacement

Not every shower pan leak requires a tear-out. A cracked drain flange or failed drain seal is sometimes repaired from above. A punctured liner under a mortar bed usually needs the tile and mortar removed so the liner can be replaced or patched according to the manufacturer's guidance.

A contractor should explain which approach fits your shower, what is included and how the repair will be tested. Ask whether the plan includes a flood test after the repair and before tile goes down.

If the shower is old, cracked in several places or built with materials that have reached the end of their life, replacement can be more reliable than a patch. Compare the scope and the cost of both options, and ask for a written explanation of each.

Shower leaks in apartments and older homes near Rutgers

New Brunswick has many multi-unit buildings and older homes where bathrooms are stacked above other living space. A leaking shower in one unit can stain ceilings and walls in the unit below, so tenants and owners should report it early.

If you plan to change plumbing or rebuild a bathroom, ask the city's Construction Department whether a permit is needed. A permit question is easier to answer before the walls are open.

If the leak has reached the ceiling below, see ceiling water damage repair. For the drying process itself, read about structural drying.

Sources for this guide: general plumbing and tile practice, the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for drying wet structures and the EPA guidance that wet materials be dried within 24 to 48 hours.
