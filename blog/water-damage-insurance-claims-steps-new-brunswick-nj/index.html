Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps in New Brunswick, NJ

A water damage claim goes smoother when you document early and keep records.

By the New Brunswick Water Damage Pros team. Published November 13, 2026.

Filing a water damage claim feels like paperwork on top of an emergency. A short checklist helps. This guide covers the order of steps, what documents matter and the mistakes that slow claims down. It is general information, since each policy is different.

Step 1: stop the water and make the home safe

Close the main valve, shut off the appliance or move people away from flooding. Most policies require reasonable steps to prevent further damage. If water is near the panel or outlets, stay out and switch off power only from a dry spot.

Step 2: document before anything moves

Take wide photos of each room, then close photos of the source, the waterline and each damaged item. Take video as you walk through, narrating what you see. Note the date and time, since the date of loss can matter.

  • Photos of the source (the burst pipe, the leaking hose, the roof stain).
  • Photos of wet floors, walls and ceilings from several angles.
  • A list of damaged belongings with approximate age and value.
  • Receipts for emergency costs such as a plumber or temporary lodging.

Step 3: notify your insurer promptly

Policies often require prompt notice. Call the claims number on your policy or app, write down the claim number, the date and the name of the person you spoke with. Ask what the insurer needs from you and whether it will send an adjuster.

Step 4: bring in a restoration company

Water extraction and drying should not wait for a final claim decision. A restoration company can start mitigation, record moisture readings and photos and give you a written scope. Ask for the scope and price before work starts. Choose a contractor who will share documentation with your insurer.

Step 5: keep a claim file

Item to keep.Why it matters.
Claim number and adjuster contact.Keeps follow-up simple.
Photos, video and failed parts.Shows the cause and the extent of damage.
Moisture logs from the restoration company.Shows drying progress.
Invoices and receipts.Supports reimbursement.
Emails and call notes.Resolves disputes about what was said.

Mistakes that slow down water damage claims

  • Throwing out damaged items before photographing them.
  • Delaying the call to the insurer.
  • Signing a contractor's assignment of benefits without reading it.
  • Making permanent repairs before the insurer inspects.
  • Not asking whether mold is covered before it appears.

What an adjuster inspection usually involves

An adjuster looks at the source of the water, the extent of the damage and the condition of materials. They may take photos, measure affected areas and ask about when you noticed the problem. Bring your photos, your list of damaged items and any records from the restoration company.

You can ask to be present during the inspection. If you disagree with the adjuster's findings, ask for the reasoning in writing and provide any additional evidence, such as moisture logs or a contractor's estimate.

Temporary repairs and living costs

Many policies cover reasonable temporary repairs, such as tarping a roof or boarding an opening, and some cover extra living costs if the home cannot be used. Keep receipts for hotel stays, meals above your usual spending and storage costs. Ask your insurer before you commit to large expenses.

If your claim takes longer than expected

  1. Check in regularly. Ask for a status update and write down who you spoke with.
  2. Send missing documents promptly. Delays often come from incomplete paperwork.
  3. Ask for deadlines. Request any dates by which the insurer must respond.
  4. Know your options. The New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance can explain the complaint process.
  5. Keep drying going. Do not pause mitigation while waiting for a decision, since delay can increase damage.

What to expect after you file

After you report a loss, the insurer assigns a claim number and often an adjuster. The adjuster reviews the cause, inspects the damage and compares it with your coverage. You may receive an estimate, a request for more documents or a decision on coverage. Keep all letters and emails together.

If payment is issued in stages, such as an initial amount for emergency work and later amounts for repairs, ask the insurer to explain the schedule in writing.

A claim checklist you can print

  • Claim number, date of loss and adjuster contact.
  • Photos and video of the source and every affected room.
  • Inventory of damaged belongings with brand, age and value.
  • Moisture logs and the restoration scope.
  • Receipts for emergency repairs and living costs.
  • Notes of every call, with dates and names.

Working with a restoration company during a claim

A restoration company handles the physical work, and your insurer handles the coverage decision. Good communication between them saves time. Ask the company whether it shares moisture logs, photos and a written scope directly with your adjuster, and sign only what you understand.

Be careful with any form that gives a contractor control of your claim or payments. Read it, ask questions and keep a copy. In New Jersey, contractors must be registered for home improvement work, and written contracts are required above $500.

A scenario: a burst pipe on a holiday weekend

A pipe bursts on a Saturday night. The owner shuts off the water, photographs the room and calls the insurer's claim line, which is open at all hours. A restoration company extracts the water the same night and sets drying equipment on Sunday.

On Monday the owner sends the claim number, the photos and the first moisture log to the adjuster. The adjuster has what is needed to start reviewing the loss. Calling right away and keeping records saved days of back-and-forth.

Hiring help after a loss in New Brunswick

After a large loss, repair contractors can be hard to find. In New Jersey, home improvement contractors must register with the Division of Consumer Affairs, and registration numbers begin with 13VH. Registration is not an endorsement, but it is a basic check you can make.

The state warns that a contractor who asks for more than a third of the payment before work begins is a warning sign. Written contracts are required for work above $500.

If your loss involves standing water, see water extraction, and if it involves wet walls and floors, see structural drying.

Sources for this guide: the New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance consumer guidance and the Division of Consumer Affairs guidance on contractor registration. Industry practice on moisture logs follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.
