Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage? New Brunswick NJ

Coverage depends on where the water came from, not just that your house got wet.

By the New Brunswick Water Damage Pros team. Published November 13, 2026.

Water damage claims turn on the cause. Two basements can have the same four inches of water and get two different answers from an insurer. One came from a pipe that burst, the other from rain that seeped through a wall. This guide explains how the common causes are usually treated and what to check before you call.

We restore water-damaged property and do not sell insurance, so treat this as general information. Your policy language and your insurer decide what is covered.

The short answer: sudden internal water versus outside water

Standard homeowners policies tend to separate water that originates inside the home from water that comes from outside. Sudden and accidental releases from plumbing or appliances are the type most often covered. Flood, surface water and groundwater are commonly excluded and need separate coverage.

Sudden and accidental water: the usually covered side

A pipe that splits overnight, a washing machine hose that fails or a water heater that lets go all share one feature. The release was sudden, unexpected and not caused by neglect. Policies often respond to the resulting damage to walls, floors and contents, though limits and deductibles still apply.

Some policies also pay for tearing out part of a wall or floor to reach the leak. Others limit that. This is a good question to put to your insurer in writing.

Flood and groundwater: the usually excluded side

The New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance explains that standard homeowners policies exclude flood. Flood coverage needs a separate policy through the National Flood Insurance Program or a private insurer. Rising river water, storm surge and water that seeps up through a basement floor generally fall here.

Gradual leaks, wear and maintenance

Slow leaks are treated differently from sudden ones. A dripping pipe that has soaked a cabinet base for months often falls under wear, tear or neglect exclusions. Policies may still pay for the resulting damage in some cases, so read the wording and do not assume.

What to do before you call your insurer

  1. Stop the water. Close the valve or shut off the appliance. Insurers expect you to limit further damage.
  2. Photograph and video everything. Show the source, the water and the damaged contents.
  3. Keep failed parts. A burst hose or fitting can help establish the cause.
  4. Read your policy. Look at the water damage, flood and sewer backup sections, and note your deductible.
  5. Call your insurer and a restoration company. Ask what documents are required before work starts.

Policy terms worth understanding before you file

Term.What it usually means.
Deductible.The amount you pay before the policy pays.
Dwelling coverage.Protection for the structure of the house.
Personal property coverage.Protection for belongings such as furniture and electronics.
Additional living expenses.Costs of staying elsewhere while the home is unusable.
Sublimit.A lower cap that applies to a specific type of loss, such as mold or sewer backup.
Endorsement.An add-on that changes what the policy covers.

Water damage claims often touch more than one of these terms. A burst pipe can damage the structure, ruin belongings and force a short stay elsewhere, and each part may have its own limit.

Renters, condo owners and landlords

If you rent, the building owner's policy usually covers the structure, and your renters policy covers your belongings. If you own a condo, the association policy and your own unit policy split the responsibility, and the dividing line depends on the governing documents.

Landlords with rental properties carry their own type of policy. Tenants who cause a loss may be asked to cover it, so tell your landlord promptly and keep everything in writing.

Questions to ask your agent today

  • Does my policy cover sudden pipe and appliance failures?
  • What is my deductible for water damage?
  • Is there a separate deductible or limit for mold?
  • Do I have sewer or drain backup coverage?
  • Should I add flood insurance, and when would it take effect?

Having these answers before a loss turns a stressful day into a manageable one.

Three common myths about water damage coverage

The first myth is that insurance covers everything that gets wet. It covers listed causes, subject to exclusions, limits and your deductible. The second is that a flooded basement is covered like a burst pipe. Flood and groundwater are treated differently from sudden plumbing releases.

The third is that reporting a loss always raises your premium. Practices differ by insurer and by state, so ask your agent how claims are treated instead of guessing.

Key takeaways

  • Coverage depends on the cause of the water and the wording of your policy.
  • Flood is usually excluded and needs separate coverage.
  • Sudden internal releases are the type most often covered.
  • Document first, call your insurer promptly and start drying right away.

Insurance questions after Hurricane Ida in New Brunswick

Hurricane Ida in 2021 pushed the Raritan River over its banks in New Brunswick, which closed Route 18 and River Road. Property owners who learned afterward that their policy excluded flood were not alone, and the lesson is to read the policy before the storm, not after.

If you are in or near the Raritan floodplain, ask your insurer or agent whether a separate flood policy makes sense. There is often a waiting period before a new flood policy takes effect.
