A wet crawl space affects the whole house, because the air under the floor rises into living areas. Water can enter after heavy rain, from a plumbing leak or through foundation vents. Emergency calls are taken 24/7, and we can talk through safe next steps for entering a flooded crawl space.

How water gets into a crawl space

Surface runoff

Downspouts and grading that slope toward the foundation push rainwater into the space.

Groundwater

A high water table pushes water up through the soil and along the foundation.

Plumbing leaks

Supply and drain lines run through crawl spaces and drip onto soil and joists.

Vent and door openings

Open vents let in rain, snow melt and humid summer air.

What a wet crawl space does to the rest of the house

Wood joists and subfloor take on moisture, and fiberglass insulation sags and loses its value. Damp air rises through gaps around pipes and wiring and raises humidity inside the home. Persistent moisture is also what mold needs to grow.

The EPA notes that controlling moisture is the key to controlling mold. A crawl space that stays damp is a standing invitation for it.

What to do about a wet crawl space: the order of work

  1. Inspect safely. Crawl spaces are tight. We check for electrical hazards, standing depth and any signs of sewage.
  2. Pump out the water. Small pumps move water to a discharge point away from the foundation.
  3. Remove ruined materials. Wet insulation, vapor barrier fragments and debris are taken out.
  4. Dry the framing. Dehumidifiers and fans run until moisture readings on joists and subfloor return to normal.

What affects the cost of crawl space water damage cleanup

Price depends on access, the depth and volume of water, how much insulation is wet and how long drying takes. We explain the scope and price before work starts. Exterior drainage and foundation repairs are separate trades.

Insulation, vapor barriers and pipes under the floor

Fiberglass insulation between floor joists holds water against the subfloor when it gets wet. It is usually removed and replaced after a flood. Vapor barriers that were torn or pushed aside are repaired or relaid once the space is dry.

Pipes and ducts in the crawl space are checked for leaks and sagging insulation. A drip from a supply line can keep the ground wet for months without any sign inside the house.

Signs that the crawl space is affecting the house

  • Floors that feel cold or soft above the crawl space.
  • A musty odor that rises through floor registers.
  • Higher indoor humidity in summer.
  • Visible moisture on pipes, ducts or the foundation wall.
  • Pests that are drawn to damp areas.

Insurance note for crawl spaces

Policies often exclude groundwater seepage and flood but may cover a sudden pipe failure. Check your policy and photograph the space before cleanup.

If water came from outside after storms, see flood damage cleanup and storm water damage cleanup. If mold is already visible, read about mold remediation. For a wet basement above the crawl space, see basement water damage cleanup.

The IICRC S500 standard addresses moisture in structural cavities, including crawl spaces. Ask your contractor how its moisture readings relate to it.
